Analysis 1 and 2

For Analysis in general, there’s a consistent method: follow your Analysis professor in the cours sessions if he/she is good. If you don’t attend the cours or you just can’t catch the informations, study on your own with YouTube (there are a lot of L1 professors there), or give the course/TD PDFs to an AI and study with it.

Analysis 1 (especially) isn’t that hard, but it isn’t that easy either. I personally think we were used to memorizing maths in high school. It’s true that we’ll see a lot of familiar material in Analysis 1, but that doesn’t mean you should ignore it. I don’t like Analysis 1, so I can’t give more advice.

Analysis 2 is much easier than Analysis 1. The most important advice: solve a LOT of integrals, like 100+ examples, and I doubt even that is enough. The DL (Taylor expansions) chapter is easy to understand. Just memorize the usual Taylor expansions so you don’t waste time in the exam. A DL exercise should be done in 25 minutes, which is what we get here.

Algebra 1 and 2

For Algebra 1 or 2, there’s only one way for me to catch up: take a look at the course, PREPARE your TD series, and FOLLOW your TD sessions. For me, it’s the only and the best way. I have no other advice.

Algorithms 1

I see Algorithms 1 as a door that opens the path for every developer. It focuses on one simple idea: problem solving. The first chapters are easy to understand. I suggest preparing the TD series. You can’t claim you’ve revised and understood algorithms if you only read the solutions and understand them. That’s why I very, very strongly recommend trying the exercises yourself, not just in your head but on paper! Every chapter is an idea, and every idea needs to be practiced well. Attending the TD without trying the exercises yourself is not enough.

Algorithms 2

Start by understanding very well what “parameterized actions” are. When you reach the files chapter, solve a LOT of file exercises. It contains ideas you can’t understand unless you try to solve the exercises yourself and work out the problems. Then, when you reach the pointers/linked lists chapter (and I hope you followed my advice about files), repeat the same process. The theory of linked lists may be hard to grasp the first time, but it requires good reasoning. Then just do A LOT OF EXERCISES to master all of Algorithms 2.

Statistics and Probability

We have 4 chapters, and all four of them are very easy. For the two statistics chapters, literally just follow your TD sessions and you’ll be fine. The probability chapters are the same as what we learned in middle school, just in a different language, and even simpler. Same advice: follow your TD sessions and you’ll do well.

OPM

OPM is one of the easiest things I’ve had in my life. It’s about something called “recursion.”

For the course side of OPM, there’s no complicated advice: 1) read your course, 2) solve examples, and that’s it. The course exercises are easier than I thought, so take it easy.

For the TP side, I strongly recommend understanding the meaning of “recursion” separately. In a TP exercise, you’ll do two things: run a code for some given variables , and write recursive functions. Follow your TP professor and you’ll be fine. It’s hard to learn the CAML language from the internet, so the best way is to give the TP PDFs to an AI so it can learn the language, and then start solving exercises.

By the way, OPM is my best module.
